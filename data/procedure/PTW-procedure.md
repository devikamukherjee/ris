# Northbank Refinery, Unit 3 — Permit-to-Work Procedure

Document NBR-U3-PTW-001, revision 4. **Fictional procedure written for a demonstration
system. It is not a real site document and must not be used for real work.**

Each numbered clause (`PTW-x.y`) is a rule that can be cited on its own. Clause text is
parsed by software, so each clause starts with a level-3 heading in the form
`### PTW-x.y Title`.

## 1. Purpose and scope

### PTW-1.1 Purpose
This procedure controls work in Unit 3 that could, if poorly planned or carried out,
release hydrocarbons or toxic gas, start a fire, or injure the people doing the work. A
permit to work is a written agreement between the person in control of the plant and the
people doing the job about what may be done, where, when, and under which precautions.

### PTW-1.2 Work that needs a permit
A permit is required for hot work, confined space entry, lifting with a crane or other
powered lifting equipment, breaking into process lines or equipment, and any cold work
inside a classified hazardous area. Routine operator rounds do not need a permit.

## 2. Roles

### PTW-2.1 Area Authority
The Area Authority (permit issuer) is responsible for the plant in which work takes
place. They issue, suspend and close permits, and they decide on every concern raised
about a permit, whether it is raised by a person or by an automated detection system.

### PTW-2.2 Performing Authority
The Performing Authority leads the work crew. They make sure every crew member has read
the permit and that the work stays within its stated scope, area and time.

### PTW-2.3 Authorised Gas Tester
Only an Authorised Gas Tester may carry out gas tests for a permit. The tester records
each reading in the permit system at the time the test is taken.

### PTW-2.4 Fire watch
A fire watch is a trained person assigned to a hot-work permit, whose only duty is to
watch for and respond to fire.

### PTW-2.5 Confined space attendant
An attendant is a trained person stationed outside a confined space, who keeps count of
the people inside and raises the alarm if something goes wrong.

## 3. Issue, validity and closure

### PTW-3.1 Defined work area
Every permit marks the boundary of the permitted work area on the site plan. The work
area lies inside a single plant area and is no larger than the job needs.

### PTW-3.2 Work only within the permitted area
Work under a permit may only take place inside the permitted work area. Crew members who
need to work outside it shall stop and ask the Area Authority to amend or re-issue the
permit first.

### PTW-3.3 Validity period
A permit is valid only between its stated start and end times. Work shall stop, and the
work site shall be made safe, before the permit's end time. Work that continues after
the end time is unauthorised.

### PTW-3.4 Extension
Only the Area Authority may extend a permit, and only before it expires, after
re-checking site conditions. An expired permit cannot be extended; a new permit is
required.

### PTW-3.5 Suspension
The Area Authority may suspend a permit at any time if conditions change. Work stops at
once and does not restart until the permit is re-validated.

### PTW-3.6 Shift handover
At shift change the outgoing Area Authority briefs the incoming one on every live
permit, every open concern and every suspended permit. The handover is recorded.

## 4. Gas testing

### PTW-4.1 When gas tests are required
A gas test is required before hot work starts and before anyone enters a confined
space. Gas tests are repeated at the intervals in PTW-4.2 and PTW-4.3 for as long as the
work continues.

### PTW-4.2 Gas test validity for hot work
For hot work, a gas test is valid for 120 minutes from the time it was taken. Hot work
shall not continue on a gas test older than 120 minutes; it shall pause until a new test
is taken and recorded.

### PTW-4.3 Gas test validity for confined space entry
For confined space entry, a gas test is valid for 60 minutes. No one shall remain inside
a confined space on a gas test older than 60 minutes.

### PTW-4.4 Acceptance limits
A test passes only if flammable gas is at or below 1 % of the lower explosive limit
(LEL), oxygen is between 19.5 % and 23.5 % by volume, and hydrogen sulphide is below
5 ppm. If any reading fails, work stops and the Area Authority is informed.

## 5. Hot work

### PTW-5.1 What counts as hot work
Hot work is any work that produces flame, sparks or enough heat to ignite a flammable
atmosphere. It includes welding, flame cutting, and grinding with powered abrasive
tools. Hot work needs a hot-work permit.

### PTW-5.2 Fire watch during hot work
A fire watch shall be present at the hot-work site for the whole time hot work is in
progress. If the fire watch has to leave, hot work stops until they return or a
replacement takes over.

### PTW-5.3 Fire watch after hot work
After hot work ends, the fire watch shall stay at the site for at least 60 minutes to
watch for smouldering material, then make a final check before leaving.

## 6. Confined space entry

### PTW-6.1 Maximum number of entrants
A confined-space permit states the maximum number of people allowed inside at one time.
That number shall never be exceeded, including during crew changes.

### PTW-6.2 Attendant presence
An attendant shall be at the entry point the whole time anyone is inside the confined
space. If the attendant has to leave, everyone inside shall come out first.

### PTW-6.3 Entry and exit log
Every entry into and exit from the confined space is logged, so that the number of
people inside is known at all times.

### PTW-6.4 Attendant duties
The attendant shall not enter the confined space and shall not take on other tasks that
draw their attention away from the entry point.

## 7. Simultaneous operations (SIMOPS)

### PTW-7.1 Lifting over or near hot work
A crane lift shall not take place while its lift footprint overlaps the work area of an
active hot-work permit. Where the two cannot be separated in space or time, both permits
shall be cross-referenced and covered by a joint risk assessment approved by the Area
Authority before either starts.

### PTW-7.2 Coordination of permits
The Area Authority reviews all permits for an area together at the start of each shift
and before issuing a new permit, to find activities that could interact.

### PTW-7.3 Lift footprint
The lift footprint is the ground area under the load path, plus the crane's slewing
area, as drawn on the lift plan.

## 8. Cold work

### PTW-8.1 Cold work excludes spark-producing tools
A cold-work permit does not authorise welding, cutting or grinding with powered abrasive
tools. If such work becomes necessary, the job stops and a hot-work permit is requested.

### PTW-8.2 Non-sparking alternatives
Hand tools such as wire brushes and scrapers may be used under a cold-work permit.

## 9. Automated detection

### PTW-9.1 Advisory role
Automated detection systems in Unit 3 advise the Area Authority. They raise concerns,
but they do not suspend, close or change permits. Every concern is decided by a person.

### PTW-9.2 No personal identification
Detection systems identify people only by pseudonymous badge number and role. They do
not store images of faces or any other biometric data.

### PTW-9.3 Recording of decisions
Every concern raised, every explanation shown, and every decision made about it is
recorded with the time and the person who decided. A concern can only be dismissed
with a written reason.

## 10. Records

### PTW-10.1 Retention
Permits, gas test records, detection concerns and decisions are kept for at least five
years, in a form that shows whether any record was altered after it was made.
